Research date: 2026-09-29. Labels used: **[verified]** = revenue verified by a payment-provider-connected directory (TrustMRR says figures come straight from Stripe/LemonSqueezy/Polar and similar, not from founders); **[self-reported]** = founder interview, podcast or post; **[secondary]** = a newsletter or aggregator repeating a figure without a primary link; **[old]** = the case mostly predates 2023 and is included only as a benchmark; **[non-US]** = the founder or company is outside the US (customers may still be US or global).

Source-quality warning: search results for this topic are full of SEO listicles ("micro-SaaS ideas 2026") that quote statistics without sources, such as "68% of profitable one-person SaaS serve a vertical niche (MicroConf 2025)" and "micro-niches grew 340% (Gartner Q4 2025)". I could not trace these to a primary source, so they are **excluded** from the findings below.

## Q1. Which niches did these founders pick, and how did they discover the pain? (includes the case dataset)

### Takeaway
The niches that reached $20k-$150k/month were mostly narrow workflow tasks inside a larger professional ecosystem: Microsoft Intune app packaging, the QuickBooks Desktop API, converting bank-statement PDFs, maid-service scheduling, title-insurance quoting, feedback for ESL teachers, state-domicile tax compliance. The pain was almost always found first-hand, through the founder's own job or business, a spouse or partner's job, or repeated freelance client requests. Scraping forums was rarely how the idea came up; forums and Facebook groups were more often the *launch channel*.

#### How founders found the pain
- **Own job:** Mahony saw, during his IT consulting work, that packaging an app for Intune could take an admin up to an hour and that existing tools cost too much for small businesses — [Weekly Opportunities](https://weekly-opportunities.com/p/he-found-a-1m-year-business-hiding-inside-microsoft-s-own-ecosystem); [Stormy AI summary via search](https://stormy.ai/blog/microsoft-intune-saas-growth-playbook) (page later returned 410).
- **Own business:** Amar Ghose built ZenMaid after running a maid business and finding no good software — [Latka / search summary](https://getlatka.com/companies/zenmaid); Roy van den Broek ran an AV rental company from age 16 and found the industry using "spreadsheets held together with tape" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/building-a-15m-arr-saas-from-a-gap-he-found-at-his-brick-and-mortar-HFriCBQLHukAmdXVEj1q).
- **Spouse/partner:** Danielle, a former online ESL teacher, identified the 2-hour daily feedback burden that became FeedbackPanda — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrap-saas-arvid-kahl/).
- **Client requests (agency or freelance):** TitleCapture came from a Florida title company asking the founders' design agency for a quoting app — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/titlecapture); Tringas built StoreMapper after 3 freelance clients asked for a store locator within 2 weeks — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/micro-saas-bootstrap-tyler-tringas/).
- **Own personal need:** Angus Cheng needed CSVs from his PDF bank statements after quitting his job in 2020 — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/bank-statement-converter/); Spencer Patterson built a paywall for his own trading-education channel — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/spencer-patterson).
- **Industry tenure:** StatsDrone's founder came from years in iGaming affiliate marketing — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/ideas/software-tool/success-stories).
- **Explicit "boring niche" thesis:** Savvy Nomad's founder: "We had real demand with almost no competition" — [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/0d0c9440011c4ded). Indie Bites' takeaway from ZenMaid: an "unsexy niche (aka not selling to other indie hackers) can lead to a phenomenal indie business" — [Indie Bites](https://indiebites.com/114).
- **Sizing the niche down on purpose:** Mahony went after businesses with 100-500 devices, a segment "big enough for a full-time business but small enough that bigger vendors ignored it" — [search summary of Stormy AI / Starter Story](https://stormy.ai/blog/microsoft-intune-saas-growth-playbook).

### Inferences
- Nearly every case above $20k/month rests on first-hand insider knowledge (own job, own business, partner, or repeated client requests). None of the successful cases I found was discovered by cold forum scraping; forums were where products *launched*.
- The strongest recent (2024-2026) solo examples sit **inside a large incumbent ecosystem** (Microsoft Intune, QuickBooks Desktop) and do one painful sub-task. That gives a pre-built audience and distribution through ecosystem influencers and communities without a sales team.
- "Boring vertical" companies that grew past ~$150k/month (ZenMaid, TitleCapture, Rentman, Kibu) stopped being solo and hired 6-35+ people. The $20k-$60k band is where one person, or a very small team, is realistic.
- Named, verifiable **AI-enabled vertical** solo cases in the $20k-$150k band are scarce in public sources. The AI examples I found were anonymized (PT prior-auth), below range (AICrea $8k), horizontal (Chatbase, which has grown far beyond range at $864k MRR per [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/category/saas)), or built on one customer (Goodison).

### Gaps
- Could not verify the Bank Statement Converter figure of $38-40k MRR for 2026 against a primary source; the latest primary figure is $318k for 2024 (≈$26.5k/month on average).
- Could not get customer counts or pricing for the verified TrustMRR vertical listings (Kibu, ConvertLabs); the pages did not show them.
- The contents of the paywalled newsletter "He's making $80K/month from two boring tools" (Sep 2026) were not accessible — [Venture Radar](https://ventureradar.substack.com/p/hes-making-80kmonth-from-two-boring).
- Found no well-documented solo case built on the Clio, Procore or ServiceTitan marketplaces with public revenue.

## Q2. What price points and sales motions worked for reaching ~$30k/month?

### Takeaway
Two paths show up. (a) **Self-serve at $25-$99/month** with roughly 500-1,200 customers (Pckgr, Conductor, Bank Statement Converter, SuperX, the notary platform). (b) **Higher-ticket, sales-assisted at ~$300/month or more** with a few hundred customers (TitleCapture) or a handful of enterprise or usage-based contracts. Cases priced at $5-$15/month needed thousands of customers (FeedbackPanda 5,000; StoreMapper). Raising prices, and adding annual or enterprise/API tiers, were repeatedly named as growth levers.

### Cited Findings
- Pckgr launched free, then charged $25/mo; it now has 4 tiers up to Custom, and "price increases [were] a significant driver of growth"; 1,000+ customers at ~$60k/mo — [Weekly Opportunities](https://weekly-opportunities.com/p/he-found-a-1m-year-business-hiding-inside-microsoft-s-own-ecosystem).
- Conductor: $49/mo per QuickBooks company file plus custom enterprise pricing; 252 subscriptions produce $40k MRR, which implies an average of ~$160/subscription (so enterprise and multi-file accounts matter) — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/conductor).
- Bank Statement Converter: $30/mo, $99/mo, or $599/yr; self-serve, credit-based pricing — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/bank-statement-converter/).
- TitleCapture: ~$300/mo per title company × 1,500+ customers ≈ $300k+ MRR; sold through trade shows and underwriter partners; 0.7% monthly churn — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/titlecapture).
- Usage-based vertical AI: $5 per prior-authorization submission, ~$1k/clinic/month, ~45 clinics ≈ $41k MRR (anonymized) — [Foundra](https://www.foundra.ai/key-reads/41k-mrr-boring-workflow-solo-vertical-ai-first-time-founder-2026).
- Enterprise contract motion: $50k MRR from a single enterprise voice-AI customer — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jasonlevigoodison_bootstrapped-to-50k-mrr-solo-founder-activity-7262565413488656384-2M-b).
- Low-price plus volume: FeedbackPanda $5-$15/mo × 5,000 customers = $55k MRR — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrap-saas-arvid-kahl/); StoreMapper started at $5/mo and only later added premium analytics tiers — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/micro-saas-bootstrap-tyler-tringas/).
- Parseur cut its price from $49 to $9/mo early on to drive adoption, and is now at seven-figure ARR with ~1,000 customers — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrapped-saas-sylvestre-dupont-parseur/).
- Noosa Labs: annual plans (which reduced customers' "accounting friction") and Enterprise/API plans raised revenue per customer in acquired products — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/acquiring-micro-saas-products-and-growing-them-to-120k-mrr-uC42WHXCFk9lKt3HTPK0).
- Savvy Nomad: 3 tiers, with an onboarding quiz that shows the prospect's potential tax saving *before* showing price (value-anchored pricing) — [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/0d0c9440011c4ded).
- Transaction-fee model: Spencer Patterson's creator paywall took a fee rather than a subscription and reached $125-142k MRR at 95% margins — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/spencer-patterson).
- Shopify context: average app cost is $58-$67/mo; 40%+ of apps offer free plans or trials — [Craftberry](https://craftberry.co/articles/shopify-app-store-statistics).

### Inferences
- Rough arithmetic to $30k MRR: ~1,000 × $30, ~300 × $100, ~100 × $300, or ~30 × $1,000. Self-serve cases in the data cluster at $25-$99, which means around 500-1,200 customers. That is achievable only with a no-touch channel (SEO, app store, community).
- Cases at $300/month or more used trade shows, partner/underwriter channels or in-person visits: slower to start, but churn is lower (0.7%/month at TitleCapture).
- No case in the data mentioned setup fees, so there is no evidence either way on them.
- Pricing per unit of value (per company file, per page credit, per submission, per device tier) appears repeatedly; it lets revenue scale with customer size without a sales team.

### Gaps
- Little public data on annual-contract share or setup fees for these solo cases.
- No reliable conversion-rate or CAC data for these specific cases, apart from Parseur's Zapier conversion and the anonymized walk-in claim.

## Q3. How long did it take, and what were the main acquisition channels?

### Takeaway
Time to $20k-$60k MRR ranged from ~6 months (audience-led SuperX) through ~2-4 years (FeedbackPanda, Bank Statement Converter, Conductor) to a decade (ZenMaid to $200k). The channels that worked were **communities where the niche already gathers** (subreddits, Facebook groups), **ecosystem influencers** (Microsoft MVP YouTube demos), **SEO on narrow high-intent queries**, **app stores and integration directories** (Shopify, Zapier, Slack), and, for higher-ticket verticals, **trade shows and partner referrals**.

### Cited Findings
- Pckgr: free launch in Intune subreddits and Facebook groups, then YouTube demos from Microsoft MVPs became "a primary growth channel" — [Weekly Opportunities](https://weekly-opportunities.com/p/he-found-a-1m-year-business-hiding-inside-microsoft-s-own-ecosystem).
- Bank Statement Converter: first sale ~1 month after launch; ~$10k in year 1, $49k in 2022, $147k in 2023, $318k in 2024; traffic "primarily from Google search," with no ad spend after early ads failed — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/bank-statement-converter/); [X](https://x.com/BallerIndustry/status/1902529043653951745).
- Conductor: founded Jul 2022, $40k MRR by 2026 [verified] — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/conductor).
- TitleCapture: revenue in ~3 months after launching at the ALTA trade show; underwriter partner emails had 80% open rates — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/titlecapture).
- FeedbackPanda: ~2 years from launch to $55k MRR and exit; growth came from teacher Facebook groups (a single comment brought ~100 signups on day one) — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrap-saas-arvid-kahl/).
- StoreMapper: $1k MRR after ~9 months as a side project; the Shopify App Store was the main engine; also intercepted Upwork job posts — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/micro-saas-bootstrap-tyler-tringas/).
- Parseur: ~95% of traffic from SEO; the Zapier integration converts at 20-30% — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrapped-saas-sylvestre-dupont-parseur/).
- Rentman: word-of-mouth, with freelance crew members asking for the tool at their next employers — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/building-a-15m-arr-saas-from-a-gap-he-found-at-his-brick-and-mortar-HFriCBQLHukAmdXVEj1q).
- Creator paywall: 50% lifetime affiliate commission, DM outreach, YouTube influencer partnerships; profitable in 3 months — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/spencer-patterson).
- SuperX: $23k MRR in 6 months, entirely from the founder's organic X audience, after 2.5 years of posting and 5 zero-revenue products — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-23k-mrr-in-six-months-after-five-failures-4d64o9ev4AXXhX9ogHQQ).
- Noosa Labs (acquired products): paid ads and outbound "promising"; partnerships "less fruitful than expected" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/acquiring-micro-saas-products-and-growing-them-to-120k-mrr-uC42WHXCFk9lKt3HTPK0).
- ZenMaid: 11 years to $200k MRR — [Indie Bites](https://indiebites.com/114).
- Anonymized PT tool: in-person clinic visits reportedly converted at 30-50% vs ~5% for cold email [low confidence] — [Foundra](https://www.foundra.ai/key-reads/41k-mrr-boring-workflow-solo-vertical-ai-first-time-founder-2026).

### Inferences
- The fastest solo paths (≤2 years to $20k or more) combined a niche whose members gather in identifiable online communities with a launch there. Pckgr and FeedbackPanda are the clearest examples.
- SEO-led growth compounds but is slow (Bank Statement Converter took ~3 years to average $12k/month and ~4 years to exceed $26k/month).
- Trade shows and associations appear only in the higher-ticket, older vertical cases (TitleCapture/ALTA). There is little recent solo evidence for them, probably because they need travel and budget.
- Having an audience (SuperX) is a strong accelerant, but it produced a horizontal tool for other creators, which is less defensible than an insider vertical.

### Gaps
- No detailed public data from solo founders on association partnerships or trade-show ROI for 2024-2026.
- Time-to-revenue for Pckgr and ZenMaid's early years was not disclosed in the sources I could access.

## Q4. Which marketplace/integration ecosystems offer the best distribution for a new niche app?

### Takeaway
Shopify is by far the largest and most crowded (17,600+ apps, 12,100 vendors, 12.4M reviews); its developer revenue share is 0% on the first $1M. HubSpot (~2,000-2,500 apps) and Salesforce AppExchange (~5,900, flat year over year) are mid-sized. **Vertical ecosystems are much smaller and less crowded**: Procore ~539 apps from 439 vendors, Clio 250+ integrations, QuickBooks roughly 170-450+ (sources conflict), and ServiceTitan only 14 *certified* partners as of June 2026. That points to less competition but gated access in vertical marketplaces. The solo cases show that the ecosystems which *worked* in practice included unofficial "ecosystem gaps", such as Intune and QuickBooks Desktop, as well as official app stores.

### Cited Findings
- **Shopify App Store:** 17,600+ apps as of Apr 2026 (+52% vs Q1 2025); ~564 new apps in the last 30 days; 12,100+ vendors, ~80% with only one app; 12.4M total reviews; 25.2% of apps rated 4.7-5.0; average merchant installs 6 apps; 87% of merchants use apps; Shopify paid developers over $1B in 2024 — [Craftberry](https://craftberry.co/articles/shopify-app-store-statistics). Some sources say app counts dipped in 2024 after quality tightening — [search summary](https://uptek.com/shopify-statistics/app-store/).
- **Shopify revenue share:** developers keep 100% of their first $1M gross app revenue (from Jan 1, 2025) and 85% above that; large developers (≥$20M app revenue or ≥$100M company revenue) pay 15% on all revenue — [Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share).
- **HubSpot App Marketplace:** passed 2,000+ apps and 2.5M+ active installs (Oct 2025) — [HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/Releases-and-Updates/2-000-Apps-2-5M-Active-Installs/ba-p/1209474); the marketplace listing page shows "2,524" apps — [HubSpot Ecosystem](https://ecosystem.hubspot.com/marketplace/apps/all).
- **Salesforce AppExchange:** 5,877 apps in June 2026 vs 5,951 in May 2025, i.e. flat to shrinking — [SFApps.info](https://www.sfapps.info/salesforce-apps-stats-2026/).
- **Procore App Marketplace:** 539 products from 439 vendors — [AppMarketplace.com](https://appmarketplace.com/marketplaces/procore-app-marketplace/).
- **Clio App Directory:** "250+ apps" — [Clio](https://www.clio.com/features/integrations/); the directory runs to 41 pages — [Clio App Directory](https://www.clio.com/app-directory/).
- **QuickBooks App Store:** sources conflict. One tracker lists 170 apps — [AppMarketplace.com](https://appmarketplace.com/marketplaces/quickbooks-app-store/); another source says 450+ solutions — [search summary of Alphaserve/QBcure](https://www.alphaservesp.com/blog/quickbooks-app-store-the-ultimate-guide).
- **ServiceTitan Marketplace:** relaunched with app certification; 14 partners certified (accounting, payroll, POS, ecommerce, CSR) as of a June 2026 update; 120+ companies applied in 3 months (as of Sep 2025) — [ServiceTitan blog](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/app-marketplace-certified-apps); [ServiceTitan relaunch](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/app-marketplace-relaunch).
- **Integration as a channel:** Parseur's Zapier listing converts 20-30% of referred users — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrapped-saas-sylvestre-dupont-parseur/).
- **Ecosystem gap as a product:** Conductor sells a modern API for QuickBooks Desktop to *vertical SaaS companies* ($40k MRR verified), showing that legacy accounting integrations are painful enough to pay for — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/conductor); [Conductor](https://conductor.is/).
- **Microsoft ecosystem:** Pckgr grew through Microsoft MVPs and Intune admin communities rather than through a Microsoft store listing — [Weekly Opportunities](https://weekly-opportunities.com/p/he-found-a-1m-year-business-hiding-inside-microsoft-s-own-ecosystem).
- **Historical Shopify effect:** StoreMapper grew by being the only store locator in the Shopify App Store — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/micro-saas-bootstrap-tyler-tringas/). Kaching's portfolio exceeds $100k MRR on Shopify — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/getting-out-of-the-freelancing-game-by-building-a-100k-mrr-shopify-app-portfolio-qdReVAgLjz6EpW4OrJSI).

### Inferences
- Shopify has proven, fast distribution but is saturated. With 17,600 apps and ~560 new ones a month, being "the only X in the app store", as StoreMapper was, is far less likely today. A new niche app would need a category with few competitors, or a sub-vertical of merchants.
- Vertical marketplaces (Procore, Clio, ServiceTitan) have 1-2 orders of magnitude fewer apps, so a new app faces less competition. But ServiceTitan's certification gate, and enterprise-leaning customers in Procore and Clio, mean slower approval and longer sales cycles. No solo revenue cases on these marketplaces surfaced publicly, which is itself a signal of uncertainty.
- Unofficial ecosystems (Microsoft Intune admins, QuickBooks Desktop developers) can be the best distribution of all. Communities and influencers (MVPs, consultants) function as a de facto marketplace with no listing competition.

### Gaps
- Could not get review-count distributions for the Clio, Procore, QuickBooks or ServiceTitan marketplaces; no data found on install volumes per app in vertical marketplaces.
- No revenue-share data gathered for Procore, Clio, ServiceTitan or QuickBooks.

## Q5. What failure patterns show up?

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are **platform dependence** (terms-of-service or cease-and-desist risk, app-store dependence that caps growth), **price-sensitive niches with revenue shocks**, **solo burnout from never hiring**, **customer concentration** (enterprise single-customer MRR), **long sale processes and low exit multiples**, and a **plateau early** for thin AI wrappers aimed at crowded prosumer verticals.

### Cited Findings
- Platform/ToS risk: Noosa Labs' WhatsApp-based WAMessages received a cease-and-desist and needed a nine-month recovery. Its founder now avoids building on Meta, LinkedIn and X: "doing something against the TOS of a large platform is definitely not acceptable risk" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/acquiring-micro-saas-products-and-growing-them-to-120k-mrr-uC42WHXCFk9lKt3HTPK0).
- App-store dependence caps growth: Tringas "never discovered scalable paid acquisition channels and remained dependent on organic platform discovery" on Shopify, and sold for "level up money" — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/micro-saas-bootstrap-tyler-tringas/).
- Price-sensitive niche and revenue shock: ZenMaid (maid services, price-sensitive) at one point "los[t] 40% of revenue in 6 months" — [Indie Bites](https://indiebites.com/114).
- Solo burnout: Arvid Kahl said never hiring was FeedbackPanda's biggest regret; on-call anxiety persisted a year after the exit — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrap-saas-arvid-kahl/). Noosa: "doing all of it by myself would be a way to burnout" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/acquiring-micro-saas-products-and-growing-them-to-120k-mrr-uC42WHXCFk9lKt3HTPK0).
- Customer concentration: $50k MRR from one enterprise customer — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jasonlevigoodison_bootstrapped-to-50k-mrr-solo-founder-activity-7262565413488656384-2M-b).
- Many zero-revenue attempts before a hit: Rob Hallam had 5 products earning $0 over 2.5 years — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-23k-mrr-in-six-months-after-five-failures-4d64o9ev4AXXhX9ogHQQ); Kaching's founder had "several failed projects" first — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/getting-out-of-the-freelancing-game-by-building-a-100k-mrr-shopify-app-portfolio-qdReVAgLjz6EpW4OrJSI).
- Market dependence on a single upstream industry: FeedbackPanda's niche was freelance teachers at Chinese online schools, a market that grew from 5,000 to ~75,000 teachers. It depended entirely on that sector — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrap-saas-arvid-kahl/). (The later regulatory crackdown on that sector is not covered in this source.)
- Too many verticals at once: Data Talks targeted sports, retail and utilities but found product-market fit only in sports — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/stefan-laven-datatalks-325/) (title and search summary).
- Competing with funded players: FE International notes that small SaaS in highly competitive niches "tend to find themselves underfunded and unable to compete" with VC-backed rivals — [FE International](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/saas-metrics-value-saas-business) (via search summary).
- Weak moats in utility tools: Bank Statement Converter's interview cites no structural defensibility beyond bank-format coverage — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/bank-statement-converter/). Competitors now claim to build such converters with AI agents "in one day" — [Bank Statement Sheet](https://bankstatementsheet.com/blog/how-we-built-bank-statement-converter).
- Long exit processes: the creator paywall sale took ~18 months — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/spencer-patterson); a $1.3M Flippa SaaS deal took 250 days — [Flippa](https://flippa.com/blog/2025-online-business-ma-insights-from-flippa/).

### Inferences
- AI makes it cheaper to build narrow utilities, and so cheaper to clone them. Defensibility has to come from workflow depth, compliance or accuracy engines, integrations, or community position rather than from the feature itself.
- Niches with low willingness to pay per seat (maid services, ESL teachers) can still work, but only at high volume and with churn exposure.

### Gaps
- Found few documented *failed* solo vertical SaaS post-mortems with numbers (too-small market, no budget) from 2024-2026; the evidence above is mostly lessons from survivors.

## Q6. What does small niche SaaS sell for on acquisition marketplaces?

### Takeaway
Micro-SaaS trades on profit (SDE), not ARR. Acquire.com's median is ~3.9x SDE (2024 and 2025). Flippa's 2025 average is 2.7x profit (top quartile 5.8x). Empire Flippers uses 30-50x *monthly* net profit (≈2.5-4.2x annual). Small deals (<$100k) close at under 2x profit. Named exits in the case set cluster around **~2-3x ARR**. Flippa and brokers report the strongest buyer demand for vertical SaaS and AI-augmented workflow tools, and SaaS deal volume rose sharply in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Acquire.com: 3.9x median profit multiple in both 2024 and 2025; businesses under $100k net income averaged ~3.7x and those at $100k-$1M ~3.9x (the 2025 report covered micro-SaaS under $1M TTM revenue) — [Acquire.com multiples report Jan 2026](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/) (figures via search summaries; the page and PDF did not render for me); [Acquire annual report 2025](https://blog.acquire.com/annual-saas-report-2025/).
- Flippa 2025: SaaS transactions up 73.5% YoY; average profit multiple 2.7x, top quartile 5.8x; Flippa expects the "strongest buyer demand" in 2026 for vertical SaaS and AI-augmented workflow tools — [Flippa 2025 recap](https://flippa.com/blog/2025-online-business-ma-insights-from-flippa/).
- Flippa multiples by category: Marketing Tools 5.9x profit / 2.1x revenue; Education 4.1x / 3.2x; Health & Wellbeing 3.9x / 1.8x; Internet & Security 3.4x / 1.9x; CRM Tools 2.6x / 2.1x. Flippa had facilitated 795 SaaS sales by end of 2024; preferred churn is below 6% — [Flippa SaaS multiples](https://flippa.com/blog/saas-multiples/).
- Example Flippa sales: AI EdTech at $29,074 MRR sold for $575K (1.7x revenue, 4.2x profit); call tracking at $18,116 MRR sold for $725K (2.9x revenue, 4.0x profit); sports data analytics at $18,380 MRR sold for $720K (2.4x revenue, 2.8x profit) — [Flippa SaaS multiples](https://flippa.com/blog/saas-multiples/).
- Flippa by deal size: $10K-$100K deals close at ~1.68x profit, $500K-$1M at 2.18x, $1M+ at 2.43x — [Flippa](https://flippa.com/blog/saas-multiples/) (via search summary; may be older data).
- Empire Flippers: 12-month average net profit × 30-50x monthly multiple for SaaS (≈2.5-4.2x annual) — [Empire Flippers](https://empireflippers.com/saas-company-valuation-multiples-metrics/) (via search summary).
- Named exits: Kaching's first Shopify app at $6.5k MRR sold for $250k (≈3.2x ARR, 2021) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/getting-out-of-the-freelancing-game-by-building-a-100k-mrr-shopify-app-portfolio-qdReVAgLjz6EpW4OrJSI). Creator paywall at $125-142k MRR sold for $3.5M (≈2.1-2.3x ARR, late 2023) — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/spencer-patterson). FeedbackPanda at $55k MRR sold for "seven figures" (≥1.5x ARR, 2019) — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/bootstrap-saas-arvid-kahl/).
- Buyer criteria as a durability signal: Noosa Labs buys $200-600k ARR SMB SaaS with 50%+ margins and product-led growth — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/acquiring-micro-saas-products-and-growing-them-to-120k-mrr-uC42WHXCFk9lKt3HTPK0). On Axial it lists $200-800k ARR and 60%+ SDE margin — [Axial](https://www.axial.net/company/noosa-labs-inc/).
- TrustMRR, a verified-revenue directory that doubles as a marketplace, lists 10,573 startups — [TrustMRR search summary](https://trustmrr.com/startups).
- Market context: 2,698 SaaS M&A deals in 2025, up 28% from 2024; Q3 2025 set a record of 746 — [search summary citing Flippa guide](https://flippa.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-saas-mergers-and-acquisitions/) (not independently verified).

### Inferences
- A solo niche SaaS at $30k MRR with ~80% SDE margin (~$290k SDE) would, at 2.7-3.9x, plausibly sell for ~$0.8-1.1M. Top-quartile profile (low churn, growth) could approach 5-6x.
- Categories with the highest Flippa profit multiples (marketing tools, education) are not the "boring vertical" ones. But brokers explicitly flag vertical SaaS as the 2026 demand leader. Niche-specific multiples are not published in enough detail to rank verticals.
- Low churn (<6%/month preferred; TitleCapture 0.7%; FeedbackPanda <3%) is the main valuation lever that vertical tools tend to have.

### Gaps
- Could not access the Acquire.com Jan 2026 report or the 2025 PDF directly to confirm figures, breakdowns by B2B vs B2C, or AI-business multiples.
- No marketplace publishes multiples by fine-grained vertical (e.g., trades vs legal vs healthcare compliance), so evidence on which niches carry durable revenue remains indirect.
