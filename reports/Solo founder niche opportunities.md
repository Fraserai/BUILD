# Chase deadlines, not chatbots, to $30k/month

The most realistic route to about $1,000/day in profit for a solo founder starting in late 2026 is not a better AI feature. It is owning a dull, deadline-driven document workflow that incumbents price for enterprises. The top pick here is an AI pipeline with human quality checks that turns the scanned agendas, minutes, budgets and notices of small governments and special districts into accessible WCAG 2.1 AA documents. Those entities face DOJ's ADA Title II deadlines of **April 26, 2027** (population 50,000 or more) and **April 26, 2028** (smaller entities and all special districts). The popular AI-SaaS ideas the user is right to distrust (receptionists, missed-call text-back, note-takers, chatbots, headshots, writers, resume builders) are being absorbed by platforms that already own the customer. Zoom now sells a receptionist for **$24.99–$29.99/month** that works on any phone system, and Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan build one into their field-service software. The ranked shortlist favors niches that combine three things: a regulatory or financial forcing function, a legacy PDF, fax or spreadsheet workflow, and a price gap below enterprise tools. In ranked order: small-government document accessibility, certificate-of-insurance issuance for small commercial insurance agencies, multi-state packaging EPR and PFAS reporting for Shopify-scale brands, small commercial property tax appeals on contingency, fax referral intake for small medical-equipment and home health providers, refrigerant leak records sold through HVAC contractors, and utility bill audits for multi-site operators. At about $250/month, **roughly 150 customers produce about $37.5k in monthly recurring revenue (MRR)**. That covers an estimated $5–8k of monthly costs and leaves about $30k profit. The evidence is strongest on the regulatory side (Federal Register rules, Census counts) and weakest on willingness to pay. No niche here has been tested in real customer conversations, several workflow statistics come from vendor blogs, and the public case studies show that solo founders who reach $20–60k/month almost always start with insider knowledge. The next 4–6 weeks should go to validation, not code.

## Platforms are turning the popular AI-SaaS ideas into free add-ons

The user's rejection of "AI missed-call text-back for contractors" holds up against the market data. Missed-call text-back now costs **$15–29/month at the low end**, and the same feature comes inside HighLevel ($97–497/month), Weave (from $199), Podium ($249–399) and Birdeye ($349 per location) ([Sonant](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/missed-call-text-back); [Recoverly](https://recoverlyhq.com/blog/best-missed-call-text-back-software-2026)). The fight has moved to SEO. Vendors publish near-identical "best missed-call text-back for cleaners / landscapers / contractors" pages ([Service Business Academy](https://servicebusinessacademy.org/top-10-best-missed-call-text-back-software-contractors-2026/)). Full AI receptionists are worse. Zoom launched a standalone Virtual Agent Receptionist on July 9, 2026 at $29.99/month for 100 minutes ([Zoom](https://news.zoom.com/standalone-zoom-virtual-agent-receptionist/)). Beside raised **$32M** to build the same thing for small businesses ([Yahoo Finance/Fortune](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-beside-ai-voice-startup-130000825.html)). Jobber sells an AI receptionist as a $99 add-on, and Housecall Pro includes one at no extra charge ([theaitrades.ai](https://theaitrades.ai/blog/jobber-ai-vs-servicetitan-ai-vs-housecall-pro-ai); [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/ai-team/)). Turnkey "AI voice receptionist reseller" businesses are even listed for sale on BizQuest ([BizQuest](https://images.bizquest.com/start-up-business/turnkey-ai-voice-receptionist-reseller-opportunity/BW2400009)). When a product is sold as a franchise kit, it has become a commodity.

The failure mechanism is structural, not a matter of execution. The feature becomes a checkbox in a product the customer already pays for: the model provider, the meeting or phone platform, or the vertical system of record. OpenAI's native file upload wiped out a wave of "ChatGPT for PDFs" startups ([MachineBrief](https://www.machinebrief.com/news/death-of-ai-wrapper-startups-wont-survive-2026)). Model inference prices fell **more than 280-fold** between November 2022 and October 2024 ([The Innovation Attorney](https://theinnovationattorney.com/proprietary-data-moats-and-ai-startup-defensibility-in-2026/)), which erased any product whose only margin was the gap between API cost and price. AI coding also removes the solo founder's supposed edge. Rob Walling notes that prosumers will "blow a weekend coding something" rather than pay a $100/year subscription ([Startups For the Rest of Us Ep. 842](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-842-what-is-the-future-of-saas-in-an-ai-world-rob-solo)). The baseline odds are poor: about **70% of independent SaaS founders earn under $1,000 MRR** ([Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/)). At $30–100/month, $30k/month profit needs 300–1,000 customers competing against free or bundled alternatives, and nothing in the evidence suggests a solo founder can win that fight.

Several "recovery" gold rushes that look attractive from the outside have already closed or been automated. Amazon cut the FBA claim window to **60 days** and since March 2025 values reimbursements at manufacturing cost rather than sale price, while GETIDA, Refunds Manager and Seller Investigators all charge the same 25% ([SPS Commerce](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/amazon-reimbursement-policy); [Forceget](https://forceget.com/blog/amazon-reimbursement-policy-update-2025/); [AMZFinder](https://www.amzfinder.com/blog/getida-review/)). Chargeflow automates chargeback disputes for 25% of wins ([Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/pricing)), and Reveel recovers parcel late-delivery credits for free ([Reveel](https://reveelgroup.com/solutions/parcel-audit-solution/)). IEEPA tariff refunds are real, but about **$122B of a roughly $166B pool had already been certified by September 11, 2026** ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/24/the-u-s-is-refunding-122-billion-in-trump-tariffs-heres-who-gets-the-money/)), and filing requires the importer of record or a licensed customs broker ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2026/02/how-to-request-tariff-refunds.html)). Four other windows are gone or on hold. The retroactive Section 174 election window closed July 6, 2026 ([Cherry Bekaert](https://www.cbh.com/insights/alerts/rev-proc-2025-28-new-guidance-on-section-174a/)). WOTC lapsed for new hires after December 31, 2025 ([UHY](https://uhy-us.com/insights/news/2025/december/work-opportunity-tax-credit-wotc-expiration-extension-and-what-employers-need-to-know)). Beneficial-ownership (BOI) reporting for domestic companies was permanently eliminated on August 14, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision)). The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250801-eighth-circuit-vacates-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-but-federal-and-state-regulators-likely-to-remain-active)). None of these should anchor a 12–24 month business.

## Durable niches share a deadline, a legacy file format and a labor budget

Investors and bootstrappers agree on where defensibility has moved. Bessemer argues vertical AI wins by automating language-heavy workflows with a clear wedge, deep domain context and immediate 10x ROI. It also describes "Trojan horse" products that capture data flowing out of legacy systems without replacing them ([BVP State of AI 2025](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-state-of-ai-2025)). a16z makes the budget argument. US software spend of **$313B was about 3% of the $10.5T spent on labor** in 2022, so products that sell finished work reach a far larger pool than products that sell seats ([a16z](https://a16z.com/vertical-saas-now-with-ai-inside/)). Walling puts it bluntly: "The moat was never the code". The defensible categories need structured data, compliance and durability ([SFTRU Ep. 842](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-842-what-is-the-future-of-saas-in-an-ai-world-rob-solo)). Jason Cohen adds that AI "for experts" beats AI "for novices", because experts can fix output that is 80% right, and that founders should aim for 3–10x improvements rather than 20% ([SFTRU Ep. 817](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-817-bootstrapping-in-the-age-of-ai-with-jason-cohen)). YC's 2026 Requests for Startups point the same way. They ask for AI-native service companies in insurance brokerage, accounting, compliance and healthcare administration, AI-native compliance infrastructure, and operating systems for physical-world industries that spend "10 to 100x more on labor than software". None of them asks for receptionists, note-takers or chatbots ([YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs); [The VC Corner](https://www.thevccorner.com/p/yc-summer-2026-requests-for-startups-ideas)).

Real solo and small-team outcomes support this. Pckgr packages Windows apps for Microsoft Intune, aimed at IT teams managing 100–500 devices. It reached about **$60k/month with 1,000+ customers**, launching in Intune subreddits and growing through YouTube demos by Microsoft MVPs ([Weekly Opportunities](https://weekly-opportunities.com/p/he-found-a-1m-year-business-hiding-inside-microsoft-s-own-ecosystem)). Conductor sells a modern API for the legacy QuickBooks Desktop and has **$40,181 verified MRR from 252 subscriptions**, about $160 average, with the founder reporting "virtually no churn" ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/conductor)). TitleCapture charged title companies about $300/month, launched at the industry trade show, and runs **0.7% monthly churn** ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/titlecapture)). The pattern is consistent. Each founder solved one painful sub-task inside an existing professional ecosystem. The pain came from the founder's own job, business, spouse or repeated client requests, not from scraping forums. Online communities served as launch channels, not idea sources. Founders who grew past about $150k/month hired 6–35 people, which puts **$20–60k/month in the realistic band for one person**. Exits are real but modest. Acquire.com's median is about 3.9x profit, and Flippa's 2025 average was 2.7x, with vertical SaaS and AI-augmented workflow tools flagged as 2026's strongest buyer demand ([Acquire.com](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/); [Flippa](https://flippa.com/blog/2025-online-business-ma-insights-from-flippa/)). On those multiples, a $30k/month-profit business is plausibly worth $1M or more.

The case data also sets the pricing math. About $30k MRR takes roughly 1,000 customers at $30, 300 at $100, 100 at $300, or 30 at $1,000. Self-serve products at $25–99 needed a no-touch channel such as SEO or an app store. Products at $300 or more used trade shows, partner channels or in-person sales, which were slower to start but churned less ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/titlecapture); [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/bank-statement-converter/)). From this evidence, the report screened every candidate on five tests. Does a hard date or a money-recovery motive force the purchase? Is there a legacy PDF, fax or spreadsheet workflow that AI can do 3–10x faster? Is there a price gap below enterprise vendors? Can the founder reach buyers through associations or communities? And can the business get to $36–40k in monthly revenue with 200 or fewer customers?

### 1. Small-government document accessibility has the clearest date and the widest price gap

DOJ's interim final rule of April 20, 2026 pushed the Title II web and mobile deadlines back one year, to **April 26, 2027 for entities serving 50,000 or more and April 26, 2028 for smaller entities and all special districts**. It explicitly kept **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** as the standard ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/20/2026-07663/extension-of-compliance-dates-for-nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web); [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/doj_extends_ada_title_ii_digital_accessibility_deadlines_one_year_0426.html)). Covered content includes websites, online forms, **PDFs**, e-filing portals and apps, for "state and local governments… courts and special districts (such as water, fire protection and transit authorities)" ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/doj_extends_ada_title_ii_digital_accessibility_deadlines_one_year_0426.html)). The buyer pool is large and mostly tiny: **90,837 local governments**, including **39,555 special districts** and 35,705 municipalities and townships ([St. Louis Fed/Census](https://www.stlouisfed.org/publications/regional-economist/2024/march/local-governments-us-number-type)). HHS added a matching Section 504 rule for HHS-funded providers, with deadlines of May 11, 2027 (15 or more employees) and **May 10, 2028** (fewer than 15), which opens a second segment of small clinics ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-extends-mobile-and-web-accessibility-deadline.html)).

The incumbents are priced for a different buyer. Siteimprove runs about **$15k–$50k+/year**, Monsido $5k–$15k+, and Silktide about €490/month. CivicPlus charges small towns about $4,600–$4,800/year for a website and sells PDF remediation (DocAccess) and AudioEye as add-ons. Human PDF remediation services cost **$4–25 per page** ([TestParty](https://testparty.ai/blog/siteimprove-alternatives); [Town Web](https://townweb.com/blog/how-much-does-a-municipal-website-cost/); [CivicPlus DocAccess](https://www.civicplus.com/web-accessibility-software/docaccess/); [Venngage](https://venngage.com/blog/pdf-accessibility-cost/)). A water district run by a part-time clerk who posts a scanned board packet every month cannot pay enterprise monitoring prices. It could plausibly pay $150–300/month for a service that makes every upload accessible. The work also recurs: every new agenda, set of minutes, budget and notice needs remediation, so this is not a one-off cleanup. Two points in the sources cut both ways. DOJ said it had "underestimated the costs and burden" for smaller governments, which raises the odds of a further delay. Disability groups have opposed the delay and a lawsuit over DOJ's change has been reported, and the underlying Title II duty and lawsuit exposure continue regardless of the technical rule ([ADA Title III blog](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/04/doj-extends-ada-title-ii-website-accessibility-deadlines-for-governmental-entities-but-litigation-and-compliance-risks-remain/); [AAPD](https://www.aapd.com/aapd-statement-title-ii-doj-web-rule-ifr/)).

### 2. COI issuance is the highest-frequency legacy task in small insurance agencies

The Big "I" 2026 Agency Universe Study counts about **37,000 independent agencies averaging 9.9 staff**. **86% name multiple carrier interfaces as their top technology pain**, and 46% now use AI, mostly for marketing content ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/09/24/886639.htm)). Certificates of insurance are the daily grind in commercial lines. A vendor guide, citing Applied Systems and NAIC data that could not be independently verified, says COI requests consume **18–23% of CSR capacity**, take 28–45 minutes manually versus 4–8 automated, and that a mid-size agency issues 300–500 a month ([US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/certificate-of-insurance-request-handling-for-agencies-guide-2026)). The product would read the certificate inbox, identify the insured and the certificate holder, check additional-insured and waiver wording against the policy, and draft an ACORD 25 for one-click CSR approval, at $150–400/month. Competition is thickening. Kay.ai automates ACORD forms and COIs, Cara raised an **$8M seed** in April 2026, Sonant sells agency voice agents at $300–800/month, and Vertafore has added generative AI to AMS360 ([Sonant](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/100-ai-tools-for-insurance-agencies-the-complete-2026-guide); [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/04/01/cara-raises-8m-seed-to-build-ai-platform-for-brokers/); [Vertafore](https://www.vertafore.com/products/agency-management-software/ams360)). The adjacent loss-run task has already fallen to $15/user/month ([RequestLossRun](https://www.requestlossrun.com/)), which shows how fast single features get cheap in this market. The main risks are gated API access to agency management systems such as Applied Epic, and E&O exposure if a certificate implies coverage that does not exist. Human approval of every certificate is non-negotiable.

### 3. Packaging EPR and PFAS reporting grows with every new state law

Six states required producer packaging reports by May 31, 2026. California's SB 54 regulations took effect May 1, 2026, with producer registration by June 1 and a baseline report by July 1, and California is expected to exempt brands under $1M in gross sales ([Printing United Alliance](https://www.printing.org/content/2026/05/07/six-states-face-packaging-epr-reporting-deadline-on-may-31); [O'Melveny](https://www.omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/2026-plastics-epr-packaging-rules-update-what-sb-54-sb-343-state-reporting-deadlines-prc-laws-the-eu-ppwr-mean-for-producers-retailers/)). Minnesota's PFAS-in-products reports were due September 15, 2026, extendable to December 14, 2026, with an **$800 fee per report** ([Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/minnesota-extends-pfas-in-products-reporting-deadline-to-september-15-2026/)). The work is annual, per SKU and per material, and repeats state by state. That suits a product that pulls a Shopify or Amazon catalog, collects packaging weights and materials once, and produces each state's report. Shopify's revenue share is 0% on a developer's first $1M ([Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)), although the app store now holds more than 17,600 apps ([Craftberry](https://craftberry.co/articles/shopify-app-store-statistics)). This candidate has the thinnest competitor data on the list. Small-brand EPR tool pricing and the number of obligated producers were not verified, and whether small brands will pay rather than use a spreadsheet is unknown.

### 4. Property tax appeals are proven by Ownwell, but small commercial is where one person can win

Ownwell raised **$74M** to run AI-assisted appeals at **25–35% of savings**. It reports an 86% success rate but only **$774 average savings per customer** ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ownwell-property-tax-appeal-funding/); [Ownwell FAQ](https://www.ownwell.com/faqs)). At about $230 per residential win, $30k/month profit would take roughly 1,700 wins a year in a direct fight with Ownwell. Small commercial and 5–50-unit multifamily owners have savings in the thousands per property (an estimate, not a sourced benchmark), so about 50–150 clients would be enough. Retention is strong because many owners appeal every year. This is services-as-software, not SaaS. Texas requires a TDLR property tax consultant license (40 classroom hours, a sponsoring senior consultant and an exam) unless the founder is an attorney or CPA ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/)). Hearings cluster seasonally, and fees arrive only after tax bills drop. It suits a founder willing to get licensed and appear at hearings.

### 5. Fax referral intake has proven demand but is already a venture battleground

About **80% of medical-equipment (HME) orders arrive by fax**, each taking an average of **71 minutes** of back-and-forth, and about half are incomplete when first sent, according to a vendor with an interest in the numbers ([Parachute Health](https://www.parachutehealth.com/blog/ai-intake-for-dme/)). The long tail is real: about 10,000 Medicare home health agencies and 6,700+ hospices ([McKnight's](https://www.mcknightshomecare.com/news/in-2025-number-of-home-health-agencies-ticked-up-bucking-trend-new-chartbook-finds/); [MedPAC](https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Tab-I-Hospice-Dec-2025.pdf)). Willingness to pay ($300–800 per location) is the best on this list. But Tennr raised **$160M+ at a $605M valuation**, Basata raised $24.5M, and WellSky launched its own AI referral management ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/the-back-office-problem-that-explains-why-specialists-never-call-you-back/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260127232204/en)). A solo founder would carry HIPAA business associate agreements, SOC 2 expectations and EHR integration work (Brightree, WellSky) while funded companies move down-market. This is the right pick only for someone with healthcare operations background.

### 6. Refrigerant leak records ride a live rule but a low-price market

Since **January 1, 2026**, HFC appliances holding 15 lb or more of refrigerant (down from 50) require a leak-rate calculation every time refrigerant is added, repair within 30 days, verification tests and three years of records ([Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/epa-finalizes-hfc-emissions-reduction-and-reclamation-program/); [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/hvac/epa-aim-act-compliance-hvac-refrigerant-management-leak-repair)). BSI describes this as expanding federal refrigerant oversight by about 70% ([BSI](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-US/insights-and-media/insights/blogs/federal-regulators-just-expanded-refrigerant-oversight-by-70/)). It newly pulls in restaurants, convenience stores and small grocers, and they rely on their HVAC-R contractor. The EPA has trimmed related rules but appears to have left the core 15-lb requirement intact ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/the-nickel-report/status-update-on-the-aim-act-and-epas-hfc-refrigerant-regulations)). The product idea is a technician app that generates the store owner's compliance record automatically, sold per technician to contractors. Incumbent tools start at **$29/month** ([RefriComply](https://www.refricomply.com/blog/best-refrigerant-tracking-software-2026/)), so pricing power is limited. No count of appliances in the 15–50 lb band was found.

### 7. Utility bill audits are license-light but rest on the weakest evidence

Utility auditors traditionally take about **50% of refunds** and target multi-location operators spending $100k or more a year ([ICG](https://www.icg-auditing.com/about/cost); [Cost Control Associates](https://costcontrolassociates.com/utility-expense-services/cut-utility-costs/)). AI can parse bills and check them against tariffs far faster than an analyst. The model needs about 55–70 client wins a year if the average client produces about $15k in refunds and savings (a researcher estimate). The widely quoted "80% of organizations are overcharged" is a marketing claim ([Inertia Resources](https://www.inertiaresourcesinc.com/utility-bill-auditing)). Payouts come as a one-time backlog followed by a smaller ongoing share, and the sources give no average refund per client.

Several other candidates narrowly missed the list and are worth watching. FSMA 204 food traceability binds on **July 20, 2028**. The gap is capturing supplier lot data at the grocer's or restaurant's receiving door, but serious buying is unlikely before 2027 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/07/2025-14967/requirements-for-additional-traceability-records-for-certain-foods-compliance-date-extension)). The EPA Lead and Copper Rule Improvements (November 1, 2027, in litigation) hit the same small water systems that face the 2028 accessibility deadline, which makes them a natural expansion of pick #1 ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/lead-and-copper-rule-improvements)). Certified payroll carries a $13,508 penalty per missed filing, but incumbents charge $29–99/month ([CertifiedPayrollPro](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/certified-payroll-for-small-contractors)). Small freight brokerages have painful back offices: 70% of TIA members have under $2M revenue ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/fmcsa-bond-rule-may-shake-171426045.html)). But the sector is in recession, and HappyRobot raised $150M to automate brokers from the top down ([Yahoo Finance/Forkast](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/happyrobot-wants-1-2-billion-091952274.html)).

## Top pick: accessible documents for the tens of thousands of small governments facing April 2028

Small-government document accessibility wins because it is the only candidate that clears all five tests without needing a license, a HIPAA program or write-access to a gated system. The deadline falls inside the 12–24 month window. There are tens of thousands of buyers. The price gap is roughly tenfold (enterprise tools at $5k–$50k/year versus a proposed $1.8k–$3.6k/year). The workflow repeats every month. And the core labor (OCR, reading order, heading and table tagging, alt text) is exactly the multimodal document work that AI coding and vision models make cheap. The moat is not the model. It is becoming each entity's system of record for accessible publishing: its document library, its conformance log, and the evidence it can show a complaint investigator or plaintiff's attorney. Customers who route every packet through the product have little reason to leave. The honest weaknesses are that governments buy slowly, clerks are not early adopters, and the founder probably lacks insider standing in local government, which the case studies suggest matters.

### What to build first (6–8 weeks, one person with AI coding help)

The MVP should do one job very well: turn the PDFs a clerk already produces into accessible documents with an audit trail. It should not try to remediate whole websites.

| Weeks | Component | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | PDF crawler and readiness report | Given a domain, finds every public PDF, flags scanned images, missing tags, title, language and alt text, and estimates remediation pages | Serves as the lead magnet, the backlog quote and the onboarding inventory in one tool |
| 2–5 | Remediation pipeline | OCR, reading order, headings, lists and tables, vision-model alt text, metadata; outputs a tagged PDF plus an accessible HTML version; runs automated conformance checks | The core product. The HTML version gives a fallback when complex PDFs resist tagging |
| 4–6 | Human-review queue | Routes low-confidence items (complex tables, charts, forms) to a side-by-side editor for the founder or a contractor to approve | Sells as software plus service. Quality is the liability line |
| 5–7 | Email-in upload and public document library | Clerk emails or uploads a packet; accessible versions publish to a hosted, embeddable library with a per-document conformance log and exportable report | Builds stickiness and the compliance evidence customers pay for |
| 7–8 | Billing and onboarding | Stripe cards plus invoice/PO billing, annual prepay, page-based metering | Matches how governments pay |

The MVP should leave out website overlays and widgets, full-site remediation, CMS replacement, fillable-form rebuilding, video captioning and mobile apps. Those add scope and liability before the core loop is proven.

### Pricing and unit economics

The tiers below are proposals to test in conversations, not observed market prices. They sit deliberately between human per-page remediation and enterprise monitoring contracts.

| Tier | Target buyer | Price | Included volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | Special districts, townships | $149/mo | ~100 pages/mo |
| Standard | Towns of 5,000–50,000, school districts | $299/mo | ~400 pages/mo |
| Plus | Cities and counties of 50,000 or more (April 2027 cohort) | $599/mo | ~1,200 pages/mo |
| Archive backlog | Any tier | $2–3 per page, one-time | Undercuts the $4–25/page service market ([Venngage](https://venngage.com/blog/pdf-accessibility-cost/)) |

A blended average of about $250/month across **150 customers produces $37.5k MRR**, the same arithmetic the regulatory research reached independently. One-time backlog fees add cash on top. Costs should come in near the $5–8k/month estimated for comparable AI-plus-human services, covering inference, hosting, part-time QA contractors, E&O insurance and tools. That leaves about $30k profit. Per-page inference cost and the share of pages needing human review are the two numbers the prototype must measure. If review exceeds roughly 10–15% of pages, margins and the one-person model both break (a researcher estimate). Annual prepayment should be the default because it fits government fiscal-year budgeting and funds the 3–6 months before recurring revenue compounds.

### Go-to-market: the first 10 customers, then 150

The first 10 customers should come from one state, chosen for the density of its special districts and an active special-district or municipal association. The founder builds a target list from the Census of Governments, runs the crawler across 300–500 district and town websites, and emails each clerk or general manager a personalized readiness report. The report says how many of their PDFs fail, which are scanned, and what the April 2028 date means for them, and offers **free remediation of their next board packet**. The pilot is concierge-style: the founder runs the pipeline and fixes the output personally while measuring time per page. The conversion ask is an annual founding-customer contract at about 40% off for the first year, in exchange for a named reference and a case study. For faster early cash, a parallel track pitches backlog remediation to the 50,000-plus population cohort, whose deadline is only seven months away and whose budgets are larger. Many of them already pay for monitoring tools that flag problems but do not fix documents.

From 10 to 150, the channels are the ones that work for insider verticals. They include state special-district associations, municipal leagues and clerks' associations (webinars, newsletters, conference tables), much as TitleCapture used its industry trade show and underwriter partners. They also include referral partners who already serve these entities: small-town website builders, and the consultants who prepare water-quality reports and agenda packets. And they include a public, free version of the readiness scanner, which gets shared among clerks. A realistic target is 10 paying customers by month 4, 50 by month 12, and 150–200 by months 18–24 as the 2028 deadline nears. That timeline is aggressive for public-sector sales. The pipeline's value then extends into two adjacent segments: small HHS-funded clinics under Section 504 (May 2028), and water systems that must send annual Lead and Copper Rule notices.

## Five assumptions must be validated before writing code

None of the seven opportunities has been tested with buyers. The research could not reach Reddit or practitioner Facebook groups, so the pain evidence rests on trade press, rules and vendor blogs. The most important unknowns for the top pick are practical, not technical. How many pages does a typical special district publish each month? Who has authority to buy, and at what purchasing threshold does a card payment become a procurement process? Does their existing website vendor already bundle "good enough" remediation? How many have received demand letters or complaints? And can AI plus light human review actually produce documents that pass automated checks and screen-reader testing at under about $1 of cost per page? The sources contain **no data on what share of small towns already meet WCAG 2.1 AA** and no DOJ count of per-entity costs, so market size is inferred from entity counts rather than measured.

The validation plan should take 4–6 weeks. It consists of 20 conversations (10 special districts, 5 small towns, 5 entities of 50,000 or more), concierge remediation of three real board packets with every minute and failure logged, and a request for annual prepayment or a signed letter of intent at the proposed prices. The kill criteria should be set in advance. Stop or pivot if fewer than 5 of 20 will pay or sign. Stop if cost per page stays above about $1 after AI assistance. Stop if DOJ announces a further narrowing for small entities before any traction. Stop if a dominant municipal website vendor starts including remediation for free. If the idea dies, the fallback depends on the founder's own access, which the case studies show is the strongest predictor of success. A founder with insurance contacts should run the same test on COI issuance, starting with 15–20 CSR interviews about monthly certificate volume and which agency management system they use. A founder with e-commerce roots should test EPR reporting. A founder willing to get licensed should test commercial property tax appeals.

Three more risks deserve plain statement. First, rule risk is two-sided. DOJ has already delayed once and has solicited comment on the burden for small entities, but litigation over the delay could just as easily restore urgency ([ADA Title III blog](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/04/doj-extends-ada-title-ii-website-accessibility-deadlines-for-governmental-entities-but-litigation-and-compliance-risks-remain/)). Second, competition will intensify as 2028 approaches. CivicPlus already sells DocAccess to its website customers ([CivicPlus](https://www.civicplus.com/web-accessibility-software/docaccess/)), and other AI entrants will follow the same deadline, so the edge comes from being first in one state's associations, not from the technology. Third, the product must never promise legal "compliance". It should sell remediation, conformance evidence and a documented good-faith process, backed by human review and E&O coverage, because a customer sued over a document the product "fixed" would be both a liability and a lost reference.

## Conclusion

In 2026 the scarce asset for a solo founder is no longer the ability to build software. Claude Code gives that to everyone, including every competitor. What is scarce is a reason for a specific buyer to purchase this quarter and a workflow no platform has turned into a free add-on. Regulatory calendars and legacy file formats supply both. A deadline gives the founder a sales script and a date on which inaction becomes costly. A scanned PDF, a faxed order or an emailed certificate request is work that neither Zoom nor OpenAI will absorb into a free tier, because it lives inside one industry's paperwork. The practical consequence is that the $1,000/day goal is limited by distribution in a slow-buying segment, not by product. About 150 customers at $250 is an easy product target but a hard sales target, which is why the first dollar of effort should go to 20 conversations and three concierge packets rather than to a codebase.

The pricing lesson matters as much as the niche. The candidates that clear $30k/month with fewer than 200 customers all charge for completed work (documents made accessible, certificates issued, refunds recovered) and draw on the buyer's labor budget, not its software budget. That is the services-as-software framing investors describe, scaled down to one person. It also explains why the rejected missed-call idea fails. It sells a feature at a software price into a market where the phone system, the field-service platform and Zoom all give that feature away.
